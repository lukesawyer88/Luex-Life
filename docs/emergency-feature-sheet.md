# Luex Life – Contacts and Emergency feature sheet

Agreed 27 Oct 2026. Module 9 of 15 (everyone). Research: ICE (In Case of Emergency), Medical ID Records, the phone's own Medical ID, Trustworthy, FamilyWall, printed babysitter sheets.

## Owner's decisions
1. Simple on purpose: who to call and what to do, for everyone in the house and the babysitter.
2. Never replaces 999 or 112, and says so.
3. Nothing is connected to other apps; contacts can be added from a contacts file (vCard) exported from a phone.
4. Decided the recommended way (owner said go ahead): allergies and medicines are seen by the whole household; each grown-up chooses whether their medical conditions are shown; the babysitter link needs no sign-in and runs out (up to a month); the Wi-Fi password only goes in a babysitter link when ticked.
5. Push notifications only, as for the rest of the app.

## Built (must have)
1. Emergency screen: big "Call 999" and "Call 112" buttons, the home address in large text to read out, and "who to call" in order with call buttons.
2. An emergency card for each person: allergies, medicines, medical conditions, blood group, GP and surgery phone. Each grown-up keeps their own; parents keep the children's and anyone who doesn't sign in.
3. Household contacts: family, medical, school, home and repairs, neighbours, other; tap to call, email or text; notes.
4. Home essentials: where the stopcock, fuse box, gas meter, first-aid kit and spare key are; the Wi-Fi.
5. Works without a signal: the emergency screen, cards and contacts are kept on the phone.
6. Babysitter link: no app or sign-in, runs out on the date chosen; shows the children's and pets' cards, who to call, the home essentials and bedtimes (never grown-ups' cards or hidden conditions).
7. Print an emergency sheet for the fridge.
8. A clear note that it never replaces 999 or 112.

## Built (small extras)
1. Add contacts from a contacts file (vCard).
2. A nudge in April and October: "Are your emergency details still right?"
3. Pets: vet, microchip number and insurer.
4. A family plan: where to meet if separated, and who collects the children.

## Not built (could be added later)
1. A guide to setting up the phone's own Medical ID (so details show on the lock screen).
2. "If I'm lost" cards for children.

## Left out on purpose
1. Full medical records and vaccinations (Family Health, later).
2. Insurance and ID numbers (Documents and Papers).
3. Location tracking and SOS alarms (the phone does this better).
4. Reading the phone's contacts automatically (only some browsers allow it).

## Complaints and fixes
1. One person on one phone – the whole household in one place.
2. Locked behind the phone – a printed sheet and a babysitter link.
3. Paper sheets go out of date – the link always shows the latest, and a twice-a-year nudge.
4. Everyone needs an account – the babysitter link needs none.
5. Too much to fill in – short cards; only what a paramedic or babysitter needs.
6. Privacy of medical details – grown-ups can hide their conditions; the link never shows grown-ups' cards.
